## The old clockmaker

The old clockmaker, Silas, spent his entire life repairing the ticking heartbeats of his town, yet his own pocket watch remained completely still. It was an intricate heirloom, passed down through three generations, frozen at precisely 11:11. No matter which gears he replaced or how gently he oiled the springs, the hands refused to budge. The townspeople whispered that the watch was cursed, but Silas knew it was simply waiting.

On a biting winter evening, a young woman wrapped in a frayed scarf entered his shop, holding a matching silver key she had found in her late grandfather’s desk. As she stepped forward, Silas felt an inexplicable pull and took the key, inserting it into the side of his stubborn timepiece. With a soft, musical chime, the frozen gears suddenly leaped to life, filling the quiet room with a steady, rhythmic tick.

Silas looked up, his eyes meeting hers, and realized that some things in life cannot be rushed by human hands. The watch had not been broken at all; it was merely marking time, waiting for the exact moment two separate histories were meant to intertwine.
